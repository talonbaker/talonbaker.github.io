CardFlingGame · browser mirror

Forward dealing — feel mirror

Drag off a card and flick it forward across the table. It arcs, slows under air drag, falls, lands, slides with momentum, and settles face-down. Where you grab the card decides how it spins.

This is a mirror, not the build. The real sandbox is Godot 4.7 / C#, and Godot's .NET export ships no web target — a C# project physically cannot run in a browser. So this page re-implements the same math: Layer A's gesture verbatim in 1280×720 design pixels, and Layer B's exact semi-implicit Euler integrator with the same constants. What it is good for is judging feel and dialling the numbers. What it is not is the binary — browser input timing and frame pacing differ, so treat the gesture as faithful and the timing as close.

What to look for. Grab a card dead-centre and flick straight up — it should deal flat and clean, with almost no spin. Now grab one by a corner and flick hard: fast, readable spin that survives the landing, keeps turning through the slide, and dies as it settles. The spin scales with both how off-centre you grabbed and how hard you flicked.

The held breath. Press a card and drag slowly. The card does not follow the cursor — it stays in its slot and drifts 14% of the way toward the drag, leaning as you move sideways. That partial-follow is the single most identity-defining moment in the gesture, and it is the thing to compare against Goblin, the LÖVE original.

Open question this page can answer. Spin handedness. Grab a card by its right edge, flick straight up, and note which way it turns. Do the same in Goblin. They must match — if they don't, tick Flip spin handedness and that is the answer.
