Overview

A multiplayer bicycle game where the physics is honest and the game is hard.

Bike is a small multiplayer game in the spirit of Flappy Bird, Getting Over It, and Baby Steps — but on a bike. The bike's balance comes from real bicycle dynamics, not Unity's stock rigidbody simulator. The difficulty comes from the rider's coordination problem: stay upright, pedal in rhythm, don't fall.

Why this is interesting

A real bicycle isn't balanced by the rider — it's balanced by physics. Hold it at the right speed and the bike will keep itself upright. Go too slow and it falls. Go too fast and you get high-frequency wobble. This genuinely surprising behavior emerges from a 4-body rigid-body system with no-slip rolling constraints, and it's what makes a bike interesting to ride. Capturing it correctly in a game is rarely done — most "bike" games use simplified inverted-pendulum hacks. This project uses the full equations.

The system has four rigid bodies connected by two revolute joints and constrained to roll without slipping on flat ground:

After applying the holonomic ground-contact constraint and the four nonholonomic rolling constraints, the system has three dynamic degrees of freedom:

  • Roll angle of the rear frame (lean)
  • Steer angle (front frame relative to rear)
  • Rear wheel spin angle (forward speed)

Everything else — yaw, pitch, position, front wheel spin — is determined by integrating these three plus the constraint equations.

Game vision

The eventual product is a small multiplayer game where players struggle to keep their bikes upright while pedaling around a shared world. The design pillars:

  • Intentionally hard. Hard does NOT mean unfair — the bike's balance physics is honest; the difficulty comes from the rider's coordination problem.
  • Bike feel is the acceptance gate. Every phase is blocked on "does the bike feel right?" before the next phase starts.
  • Physics does the punishing. No game-logic fall triggers. The bike falls because the physics says it fell — lean past the stability envelope and the equations do the rest.
  • Performance headroom from day one. The physics runs at 250 Hz, allocation-free, at ~3 µs per step. The budget supports 100 bikes on screen simultaneously at 60 fps.

Two libraries, one game

The project is split into two pure-C# libraries and a Unity presentation layer:

Layer Project Purpose
Physics core Bicycle.Physics Deterministic, allocation-free Whipple EOM integration
World orchestrator Bicycle.World N-bike event-driven contract, no Unity dependency
Presentation unity-v2/ MonoBehaviours, rendering, input, scene wiring
