Glossary

Key terms used throughout this project. Code and prose use the descriptive English names (RearFrame, FrontWheel, etc.) — math notation from the literature appears only in derivation comments.

Four rigid bodies

RearFrame The rear part of the frame plus the rider. The rider's mass is absorbed into RearFrame; there is no separate rider body. Also called "body C" in Moore's notation or "body B/C" in Meijaard's.

RearWheel The rear wheel. Axisymmetric, rolls without slipping on flat ground. Two inertia parameters: IAxial (spin axis) and IDiametral (any perpendicular axis). Also called "body D" in Moore's notation or "body R" in Meijaard's.

FrontFrame The fork, handlebars, and any front-mounted mass. Connected to RearFrame via a revolute joint about the steer axis. Also called "body E" in Moore's notation or "body H" in Meijaard's.

FrontWheel The front wheel. Same inertia structure as RearWheel. Connected to FrontFrame via a revolute joint about the wheel's hub. Also called "body F" in Moore's notation or "body F" in Meijaard's.

Coordinate systems

Moore frame The coordinate system used by the generated equations of motion and by BodyPose.Position. +X is forward, +Y is right, +Z is down (into the ground). Named after Jason K. Moore, whose PhD thesis our derivation follows.

World frame (Unity) Unity's coordinate system: +X is right, +Y is up, +Z is forward (right-hand, Y-up). PhysicsBridge.ToUnity maps Moore (X, Y, Z) to Unity (X, Z, -Y).

Steer axis The axis about which the front frame rotates relative to the rear frame. Tilted by the steer-axis tilt angle lambda from the world vertical. For the Meijaard benchmark bicycle, lambda = 18 degrees.

Bicycle model

Whipple model The canonical mathematical model of a bicycle, consisting of four rigid bodies connected by revolute joints and constrained to roll without slipping on flat ground. Originally formulated by Whipple (1899); the Meijaard 2007 paper provides the linearized benchmark we validate against.

Holonomic constraint A constraint on positions (not velocities). The Whipple model has one: the front-wheel contact point lies on the ground plane. After imposing this constraint, the front-frame pitch is determined by the other generalized coordinates.

Nonholonomic constraint A constraint on velocities that cannot be integrated to a position constraint. The Whipple model has four: no longitudinal slip per wheel (2) and no lateral slip per wheel (2). These reduce the number of independent generalized speeds from 6 to 3.

Generalized coordinates / speeds The Lagrangian description of the system state. 8 generalized coordinates in BicycleState (XGround, YGround, Yaw, Roll, Pitch, RearWheelAngle, Steer, FrontWheelAngle) with Pitch as a dependent coordinate. 3 independent generalized speeds: RollRate, SteerRate, RearWheelSpinRate.

Self-stable window The range of forward speeds at which all eigenvalues have negative real part — the bike spontaneously returns to upright after a perturbation without rider input. For the Meijaard benchmark bicycle: approximately 4.3 m/s to 6.05 m/s.

Weave mode The unstable oscillatory mode at low speeds. A complex conjugate pair with positive real part below the low end of the self-stable window (~4.3 m/s). Manifests as a growing side-to-side wobble.

Capsize mode The mildly unstable real mode at high speeds. A single real eigenvalue with small positive real part above the high end of the self-stable window (~6.05 m/s). Manifests as a slow lean to one side.

Caster mode The heavily damped real mode. Large negative real part at all speeds. Associated with the steer angle snapping back to center — the caster effect of the front fork geometry (trail).

Kane's method

The multibody dynamics formulation used to derive the equations of motion. Chooses generalized speeds, applies virtual velocities, and produces a compact form that is efficient for both symbolic derivation and numerical evaluation. Moore's thesis derives the full nonlinear bicycle EOM via Kane's method using PyDy.

SymPy + PyDy The Python libraries used for symbolic derivation. SymPy is the symbolic algebra engine; PyDy (tools/eom-codegen/derive.py) implements Kane's method on top of SymPy's reference-frame mechanics. The output is pickled symbolic expressions passed to emit_csharp.py.

Common-subexpression elimination (CSE) sympy.cse() factors out repeated subexpressions in the EOM, reducing the FLOP count by ~10x. The emitted C# declares numbered temporaries x0, x1, ... at the top of each function.

API / runtime

BicycleSimulator The per-bike simulation object. Owns one BicycleState, one Integrator, and a cached DerivativeProvider delegate. The step cost is ~3 µs on a modern CPU.

BicycleParameters Geometry + inertia POD struct. MeijaardBenchmark is the locked benchmark preset. GameDefault is the tuning surface.

BicycleState The 11-double integrator state. 8 generalized coordinates + 3 generalized speeds. Bit-blittable — safe to use with MemoryMarshal.AsBytes.

ControlInputs The 3-double per-step torque inputs: SteerTorque, DriveTorque, RiderLeanTorque. See Gotchas 4 + 9 for the sign-convention inversions.

BicycleSnapshot The per-step output: four BodyPose values (one per rigid body) plus ForwardSpeed and SimulationTime.

BodyPose Position + orientation + linear + angular velocity for one rigid body, in the Moore world frame.

BikeWorld The N-bike orchestrator in Bicycle.World. One BikeWorld instance per scene; only BikeWorldRunner holds a reference.

BikeId A stable per-bike integer identity. Every N-bike-facing API takes a BikeId to ensure multiplayer readiness.

FeelPreset Tunable feel parameters including SteerTorqueScale and DriveTorqueScale. FeelPreset.Default matches the empirically tuned Phase 2 values (-1.5 and -3.0 respectively) that compensate for the sign-convention inversions.

Testing

Eigenvalue benchmark The pass/fail gate comparing numerically linearized eigenvalues of the simulation to Meijaard 2007 Table 2. Tolerance: <1% relative error (actual: ~1e-6). Run by EigenvalueBenchmarkTests.

Allocation-free gate AllocationFreeTests runs 100,000 Step() calls and asserts GC.GetAllocatedBytesForCurrentThread() is unchanged. Fails if any new managed heap allocation is introduced on the hot path.

Determinism gate DeterminismTests runs two simulators from identical initial states and asserts bit-identical output. Per-machine bit-exact; cross-machine bit-exactness is explicitly out of scope.
