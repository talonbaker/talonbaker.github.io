Whipple Bicycle Model

The physics core implements the canonical Whipple bicycle model — the same model used in the Meijaard et al. 2007 benchmark paper and Moore's 2012 PhD thesis. Our implementation matches the published eigenvalues to six decimal places.

The four rigid bodies

The bicycle is modeled as four rigid bodies:

                + steer axis (tilted by lambda)
               /
              /
+---------FrontFrame---------+
|           |                |
RearFrame   |         FrontWheel
|           |                |
RearWheel   *                *
*           |                |
|   ========+================+====  ground (z = 0)
            |                |
  • RearFrame — the rear part of the frame + rider (rider mass absorbed into RearFrame)
  • RearWheel — axisymmetric wheel, rolls without slipping on flat ground
  • FrontFrame — fork + handlebars + front mass
  • FrontWheel — axisymmetric wheel, rolls without slipping on flat ground

The two frames are connected by a revolute joint about the steer axis (tilted by the steer-axis tilt angle lambda from world Z). Each wheel is a revolute joint about its hub.

Degrees of freedom

After applying:

  • 1 holonomic constraint (front wheel contact point lies on the ground plane)
  • 4 nonholonomic constraints (no longitudinal slip per wheel, no lateral slip per wheel)

The system has 3 dynamic degrees of freedom:

  • \(\phi\) — lean (roll) of the rear frame
  • \(\delta\) — steer angle (front frame relative to rear)
  • \(\theta_R\) — rear wheel spin angle (sets forward speed via the rolling constraint)

Everything else — yaw, pitch, XY ground position, front wheel spin — is determined by integrating these three plus the constraint equations.

Linearized dynamics (Meijaard 2007)

Near the upright equilibrium, the lean/steer dynamics reduce to a 2-DOF linear system that depends only on forward speed \(v\) and gravity \(g\):

\[ \mathbf{M}\,\ddot{\mathbf{q}} + v\,\mathbf{C}_1\,\dot{\mathbf{q}} + \left(g\,\mathbf{K}_0 + v^2\,\mathbf{K}_2\right)\,\mathbf{q} = \mathbf{f}, \qquad \mathbf{q} = \begin{bmatrix}\phi\\\delta\end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{f} = \begin{bmatrix}T_\phi\\T_\delta\end{bmatrix} \]

Where:

  • \(\mathbf{M}\) is the symmetric positive-definite mass matrix
  • \(\mathbf{C}_1\) captures gyroscopic and centrifugal effects (scales linearly with \(v\))
  • \(\mathbf{K}_0\) is the gravity-driven stiffness/instability matrix
  • \(\mathbf{K}_2\) captures castering forces (scales with \(v^2\))

All four are constant 2×2 matrices computed once from the bike's geometry and inertia parameters.

Self-stability: the key result

The free-response eigenvalues come from:

\[ \det\left(\lambda^2\,\mathbf{M} + \lambda\,v\,\mathbf{C}_1 + g\,\mathbf{K}_0 + v^2\,\mathbf{K}_2\right) = 0 \]

Plot the real parts against forward speed \(v\) for the benchmark bicycle:

Between approximately \(v_w \approx 4.3\) m/s and \(v_c \approx 6.05\) m/s, every eigenvalue has negative real part — the bike spontaneously returns to upright after a perturbation. This is not a game mechanic; it falls out of the equations.

Full nonlinear equations (what we integrate)

The linearized form is for analysis. For the game we integrate the full nonlinear equations from Kane's method. After choosing three independent generalized speeds — roll rate, steer rate, rear-wheel spin rate — the dynamics reduce to:

\[ \mathbf{M}(\mathbf{q})\,\dot{\mathbf{u}}_{\text{ind}} = \mathbf{f}\left(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{u}_{\text{ind}}, \mathbf{T}\right) \]

Where \(\mathbf{M}\) is a configuration-dependent 3×3 mass matrix and \(\mathbf{f}\) packs gravity, gyroscopic, centripetal, and applied-torque terms.

The expressions for \(\mathbf{M}\) and \(\mathbf{f}\) are enormous — pages of symbolic algebra — but they're derived once by SymPy + PyDy, simplified with common-subexpression elimination, and emitted as straight-line C# (~3000 FLOPs, zero branches). See Build & Test Reference for the codegen pipeline.

Rolling constraints

The wheels obey no-slip rolling on the ground:

\[ v = -\omega_R\,r_R, \qquad v_F = -\omega_F\,r_F \]

The sign is a convention — forward motion produces a "negative" spin rate under our right-hand-rule choice about the wheel's spin axis. This produces the ControlInputs.DriveTorque sign-convention gotcha documented in Gotcha 4.

RK4 integration

Fixed-timestep classical Runge-Kutta 4th order:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{k}_1 &= \mathbf{f}(t_n,\,\mathbf{y}_n) \\ \mathbf{k}_2 &= \mathbf{f}\!\left(t_n + \tfrac{h}{2},\,\mathbf{y}_n + \tfrac{h}{2}\mathbf{k}_1\right) \\ \mathbf{k}_3 &= \mathbf{f}\!\left(t_n + \tfrac{h}{2},\,\mathbf{y}_n + \tfrac{h}{2}\mathbf{k}_2\right) \\ \mathbf{k}_4 &= \mathbf{f}(t_n + h,\,\mathbf{y}_n + h\,\mathbf{k}_3) \\ \mathbf{y}_{n+1} &= \mathbf{y}_n + \tfrac{h}{6}\!\left(\mathbf{k}_1 + 2\mathbf{k}_2 + 2\mathbf{k}_3 + \mathbf{k}_4\right) \end{aligned} \]

With \(h = 4\) ms (250 Hz). RK4's truncation error is \(O(h^5)\) per step / \(O(h^4)\) global; for bike dynamics (slowest modes ~1 Hz), this is wildly oversampled.

Eigenvalue verification

After implementing the full nonlinear dynamics, we numerically linearize via central differences and compute eigenvalues with MathNet's EVD routine. At \(v = 0\):

\[ \lambda = \pm 3.131642336,\; \pm 5.530940783 \quad [\text{rad/s}] \]

vs. the published Meijaard 2007 values \(\pm 3.131643247906{56},\; \pm 5.530943717653{93}\). Six-decimal agreement. This is the EigenvalueBenchmarkTests gate.

Coordinate singularity

The nonlinear EOM contains a \(1/\sin(\phi)\) factor in the dependent yaw rate (from the rolling constraint with lateral slip = 0). This diverges at \(\phi = 0\) (exactly upright). Workarounds applied throughout the codebase:

  • BicycleState.UprightStationary sets Roll = 0, but callers must perturb by ~1e-3 rad before simulating.
  • BicycleState.UprightAtSpeed sets RearWheelSpinRate correctly; callers must also set Pitch = SteerAxisTilt + 1e-5 (see Gotcha 8).
  • BikeWorld.AddBike applies both perturbations automatically (Roll = 1e-3, Pitch = SteerAxisTilt + 1e-5). See Gotcha 8.

Parameter set

The BicycleParameters struct holds the full geometry and inertia set. The static MeijaardBenchmark preset is transcribed verbatim from Meijaard et al. 2007 Table 1 and must not be changed — it is validated by the eigenvalue gate.

GameDefault starts equal to MeijaardBenchmark and is the tuning surface for feel iteration.
